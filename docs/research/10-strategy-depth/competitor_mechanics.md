# Competitor Mechanics: Strategy, Agency and Comebacks in Roll-and-Move Property Games and Their Descendants

Scope note: research done 2026-10-08. namu.wiki, mariowiki.com, modoo.netmarble.net and leagueofgamemakers.com could not be fetched directly (DNS failure from the research sandbox), so rules for those were taken from search-result summaries of those pages. Treat any number tagged "(version-dependent)" as something to confirm in the specific edition. Transferability is judged against a single shared tablet, pass-and-play, 2–4 players plus CPU, 20–40 minute sessions.

## Q1. Korean/Asian mobile and console lineage (모두의마블, 부루마블, Rich Man/大富翁, Momotaro Dentetsu, Itadaki/Fortune Street, Dokapon, Mario Party, Business Tour, Monopoly GO)

### Takeaway
The Asian lineage adds decisions on top of dice in four ways. (1) Soft dice control: the 모두의마블 power gauge, and Rich Man's motorcycle that lets you roll one die or two. (2) Hostile property mechanics: forced takeover at 2× build cost, with landmarks as the defence. (3) Alternative instant-win conditions that end the game early and force everyone to block: line, triple-colour and tourist-spot monopolies. (4) Leader-targeting NPCs and economic systems that let a trailing player profit from the leader: Momotaro's 빈보가미 (poverty god), Itadaki's district stocks. Party-style hidden end bonuses (Mario Party Bonus Stars) do create comebacks, but critics say those wins feel undeserved.

### Cited Findings

**모두의마블 (Netmarble): instant wins, takeover, landmark**
- There are three alternative win conditions besides bankruptcy. 트리플 독점 (triple monopoly): own 3 or more complete colour sets, paid at 2× victory reward. 라인 독점 (line monopoly): own every city on one of the board's 4 sides, paid at 3×. 관광지 독점 (tourist-spot monopoly): own all tourist spots, paid at 5×. — [Netmarble official guide (via search summary)](https://modoo.netmarble.net/guide/Contents.asp?depth1=324&depth2=1501&depth3=3079&id=gmm&title=)
- The number of tourist spots needed differs by version (6 in the PC online version, 5 on mobile). The PC version also has combined monopolies: triple × line pays 7×, line × tourist pays 9×. (version-dependent) — [나무위키: 모두의마블 온라인/규칙](https://namu.wiki/w/%EB%AA%A8%EB%91%90%EC%9D%98%EB%A7%88%EB%B8%94%20%EC%98%A8%EB%9D%BC%EC%9D%B8/%EA%B7%9C%EC%B9%99)
- Colour monopoly counts only ordinary cities, so tourist spots do not count toward a triple monopoly. Line monopoly does include tourist spots (operator answer collected on a board-game forum, about 4 years old). — [보드라이프: 모두의마블 애매한룰 공식답변 정리](https://boardlife.co.kr/bbs_detail.php?tb=board_community&bbs_num=47374)
- 인수 (takeover): when you land on an opponent's city you may buy it by paying 2× its current build cost. You cannot take over tourist spots or cities with a landmark. The same forum post says a takeover transfers villa, building and hotel all at once regardless of turn-count requirements, and the buyer may then add a landmark (the post may be outdated). — [보드라이프](https://boardlife.co.kr/bbs_detail.php?tb=board_community&bbs_num=47374)
- Landmark: you can build one only after a villa, building and hotel are all on the city. A landmark city is immune to takeover and to every attack, including building destruction and forced sale. Tourist spots always sell for a flat 50,000. Fortune-card forced sale and city swap can target tourist spots but not landmark cities. — [보드라이프](https://boardlife.co.kr/bbs_detail.php?tb=board_community&bbs_num=47374); [나무위키 PC rules](https://namu.wiki/w/%EB%AA%A8%EB%91%90%EC%9D%98%EB%A7%88%EB%B8%94%20%EC%98%A8%EB%9D%BC%EC%9D%B8/%EA%B7%9C%EC%B9%99)

**모두의마블: dice gauge, Olympics, doubles, world tour, island**
- Dice control gauge: you set a power gauge before rolling, and the result is biased toward its strength. A full gauge tends toward 12 and a low gauge toward 2. Control skill depends on the dice item you own and your character level. The community has tabulated outcome distributions at control 100 versus about 80. — [나무위키 모두의마블 온라인 (via search summary)](https://namu.wiki/w/%EB%AA%A8%EB%91%90%EC%9D%98%EB%A7%88%EB%B8%94%20%EC%98%A8%EB%9D%BC%EC%9D%B8?uuid=f4354371-c295-404a-bc39-f2581c6236b1); a 2013 press article on players' perception that dice are "rigged" — [머니투데이 2013](https://news.mt.co.kr/mtview.php?no=2013080215080801082)
- Olympics (올림픽): you pay to host it (1/40 of the stake, 50,000 in the beginner channel) on a city. The multiplier rises each time it is hosted, up to 5×. The beginner channel is capped at 2× and a lower channel at 3×. It stacks with festival and colour monopoly up to ×20 in the online version, and reportedly ×40 with the 바가지 (price-gouging) card. (version-dependent) — [나무위키 온라인/규칙](https://namu.wiki/w/%EB%AA%A8%EB%91%90%EC%9D%98%EB%A7%88%EB%B8%94%20%EC%98%A8%EB%9D%BC%EC%9D%B8/%EA%B7%9C%EC%B9%99)
- Doubles: three doubles in a row sends you to the island, jail or hospital (online version) or the island (Kakao version). On mobile, certain items give a chance to go to world tour instead on the third double. — [나무위키 for kakao/규칙](https://namu.wiki/w/%EB%AA%A8%EB%91%90%EC%9D%98%EB%A7%88%EB%B8%94%20for%20kakao/%EA%B7%9C%EC%B9%99); [thewiki 세부적인 룰](https://thewiki.kr/w/%EB%AA%A8%EB%91%90%EC%9D%98%EB%A7%88%EB%B8%94/%EC%84%B8%EB%B6%80%EC%A0%81%EC%9D%B8%20%EB%A3%B0)
- 세계여행 (world tour): land there, pay a fee, and next turn you move to any space you choose. A Fortune card that sends you to world tour also gives the free choice on your next turn. — [나무위키 온라인/규칙](https://namu.wiki/w/%EB%AA%A8%EB%91%90%EC%9D%98%EB%A7%88%EB%B8%94%20%EC%98%A8%EB%9D%BC%EC%9D%B8/%EA%B7%9C%EC%B9%99)
- 무인도 (island): online version, you leave by rolling a double within 2 turns, or automatically after 3. You can also use an escape card or pay. The Kakao version holds you for 3 turns. A double rolled on the turn you enter the island passes play to the next player. A double used to escape moves you but gives no extra roll. — [나무위키 온라인/규칙](https://namu.wiki/w/%EB%AA%A8%EB%91%90%EC%9D%98%EB%A7%88%EB%B8%94%20%EC%98%A8%EB%9D%BC%EC%9D%B8/%EA%B7%9C%EC%B9%99)
- Legal note: Korea's Supreme Court ruled in 2018 that 모두의마블 did not infringe 부루마블's copyright, which ended the litigation. — [MTN 2018](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2018091913574246160)

**부루마블 (Blue Marble, 씨앗사 1982)**
- The core loop is roll, move, buy land, build, collect tolls, and win by bankrupting opponents. 황금열쇠 (golden key, the chance cards) and 무인도 (island) are its signature spaces. — [게임메카](https://www.gamemeca.com/view.php?gid=97799); [보드라이프 game page](https://boardlife.co.kr/game/11240)

**Rich Man / 大富翁 (Softstar)**
- Softstar's official Richman 11 description calls cards "the original content of this series and its most strategic system." You buy cards in shops with cash. They upgrade buildings, control other players' movement, or summon gods. — [Steam: Richman 11](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2074800/); [Nintendo eShop: RichMan 11](https://nintendo.co.uk/Games/Nintendo-Switch-download-software/RichMan-11-2285819.html)
- Richman 4 cards include forced acquisition of land (not usable on corporate land), land swap, house swap, and a 7-day alliance under which allies pay no tolls to each other. — [萌娘百科: 大富翁4](https://zh.moegirl.org.cn/%E5%A4%A7%E5%AF%8C%E7%BF%814)
- Gods (神明) are NPCs that roam the board and possess players. Good gods help. Bad gods include the poverty god and the 衰神, which makes you pay double tolls. A summoning talisman calls the nearest god but can backfire. A banishing talisman removes bad gods, but not the death god. Richman 3 added large and small gods with different possession odds. — [萌娘百科: 大富翁4](https://zh.moegirl.org.cn/%E5%A4%A7%E5%AF%8C%E7%BF%814)
- Movement items: a motorcycle lets you roll one die or two. A car clears obstacles ahead (mines, roadblocks, gods). The series also has stocks (bought with bank deposits, which earn monthly interest), lottery tickets described as a way to reverse a losing position, and mini-games that earn card points. — [萌娘百科: 大富翁4](https://zh.moegirl.org.cn/%E5%A4%A7%E5%AF%8C%E7%BF%814); [Steam: Richman 11](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2074800/)

**Momotaro Dentetsu (Konami/Hudson)**
- A destination station is chosen at random. The first player to reach it collects a reward. The player farthest from the destination at that moment gets the 貧乏神 (poverty god, "Bonbi") attached, which drains their money over time. — [djcube review](https://djcube.co.uk/?p=48375)
- The god transfers when another player overlaps, passes or overtakes the carrier, so it becomes a positional hot potato. Its forms vary: a mini form takes small amounts each month, while King Bonbi throws away large sums and hands out time-bomb cards. — [Gigazine review of Momotetsu (Switch)](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20201121-momotetsu-teiban-review)
- Players also invest in property at stations. Income comes in on a monthly or yearly cycle, with a December bonus to the largest investor. Debt forces property sales. — [Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20201121-momotetsu-teiban-review)

**Itadaki Street / Fortune Street (Yuji Horii)**
- Win condition: reach a target net worth (cash + shop value + stock value) and then return to the bank first. In custom mode the target can be set from 6,000 to 999,000. — [Itadaki Street DS wiki](https://dragon-quest.org/wiki/Itadaki_Street_DS); [Giant Bomb: Itadaki Street](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Franchises/Itadaki_Street)
- Suits: collect all four suit marks (spade, heart, diamond, club) by passing their spaces, then return to the bank for a promotion. A promotion pays a fixed salary plus 10% of your total shop value plus a promotion bonus. — [Itadaki Street DS wiki](https://dragon-quest.org/wiki/Itadaki_Street_DS)
- Districts: about 4 shops each. Owning several shops in one district raises their value, prices and max capital. Owning a whole district is a "domination" with a much larger boost. You can invest cash into a shop up to its max capital. — [Mario Wiki: Fortune Street (via search summary)](https://www.mariowiki.com/Fortune_Street)
- Stocks: buy at the bank or stockbroker squares, up to 99 shares per district per purchase, and sell before your turn. Prices rise when shops in that district are expanded or invested in, or when many shares are bought at once. Heavy selling pushes them down (one guide says 10 or more shares in a turn). Shares count toward net worth. Shareholders receive a cut every time anyone pays a fee on a shop in that district. You can profit from districts owned entirely by rivals. Stocks can be turned off, and are absent in "Easy" rules. — [Mario Wiki: Fortune Street](https://www.mariowiki.com/Fortune_Street); [Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-35610/)

**Dokapon Kingdom (Sting/Atlus)**
- Atlus marketed it as the "Friendship-Destroying game." The core loop is battling, stealing from and defacing rivals. — [Gaming Nexus](https://www.gamingnexus.com/News/8839/Dokapon-Kingdom-on-a-mission-to-end-friendships)
- Movement is by spinner, and special spaces and towns need an exact landing. — [The Outerhaven](https://www.theouterhaven.net/2023/07/dokapon-kingdom-connect-review-destroying-friends-one-town-at-a-time)
- Criticism: with 2 players the stronger player can widen the gap ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/dokapon-kingdom-review)). RPGamer rated it 2.0/5 ([RPGamer](https://rpgamer.com/review/dokapon-kingdom-review/)). The full campaign takes over 25 hours ([Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/18453)).

**Mario Party (Nintendo)**
- Bonus Stars are awarded at the end of the game for in-game feats, so the apparent leader can lose. From Mario Party 7 there are 6 possible bonus categories and 3 are chosen at random each game. — [Mario Wiki: Bonus Star](https://www.mariowiki.com/Bonus_Star)
- Criticism: players who won few stars in play are "thrust to victory," which makes the win feel undeserved. CPU characters can sweep the bonuses. Critics say the hidden, late awards make strategy hard. One reviewer notes the upside that they cannot be gamed. — [Mechanics of Magic: Critical Play 7 – Mario Party](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=20809); [WhatCulture](https://whatculture.com/amp/gaming/10-frustrating-mario-party-moments-that-ruined-friendships); [Zatu review](https://zatu.com/en-au/blogs/reviews/super-mario-party-review)

**Business Tour (online)**
- The developer's FAQ describes it as a free-to-play online board game with auctions, chance events and strategic upgrades. — [Epic Games Store FAQ](https://store.epicgames.com/p/business-tour-board-game-with-online-multiplayer-faq-27319a)
- From a fan guide (may be outdated): Chance gives a random power-up, good or bad. Lost Island holds you 3 rounds, or you pay 200,000 or use an escape card. World Championship lets you choose a host city whose rent multiplier grows each time it is triggered (the same idea as the 모두의마블 Olympics). World Tour lets you move next turn to any unclaimed property, a Resort, or a property you own. Resorts cannot be bought out, and owning all 4 is an instant win. — [Magic Game World tile guide](https://www.magicgameworld.com/?p=8052)

**Monopoly GO (Scopely, mobile)**
- A dice multiplier (×1 up to ×100 or more) spends that many dice rolls in one go and multiplies the reward on the space you land on. Guides advise using high multipliers when the target is 6–8 spaces away, the most likely roll. Sources disagree on whether the multiplier also affects movement. These guides come from an item-reseller site, so trust them less. — [UDN blog guide](https://blog.udn.com/Lilidala/187128363); [Vocal guide](https://vocal.media/gamers/mm-oexp-monopoly-go-the-multiplier-playbook-for-competitive-rollers)

### Inferences
- 모두의마블's strategy comes mostly from threat and counter-threat rather than economy. Takeover at 2× lets a cash-rich trailing player break up a leader's colour set or line. Landmarks give a clear "lock it in" decision. The three instant-win conditions make every player watch every other player's sets and block or take over to stop them. All of this is visible on the board, so it works on one shared screen, and a CPU can evaluate it with simple rules ("will this purchase complete a line, triple or tourist set?").
- The Olympics / World Championship escalating multiplier is a strong comeback tool: a trailing player who owns one well-placed city can make it lethal. Its stacking (up to ×20 or ×40) is also a source of sudden, luck-driven blowouts. For a 20–40 minute tablet game, cap the stacking (for example, a maximum multiplier).
- The power gauge is the cleanest "agency over dice" mechanic in the lineage, but on mobile its fairness is perceived as opaque (the 2013 press piece on players feeling dice are rigged). If borrowed, show the outcome distribution openly (for example, low/medium/high bands with a visible spread), so it reads as a skill choice and not a hidden manipulation.
- Momotaro's Bonbi is the most distinctive comeback and kingmaking device. It punishes whoever is farthest from the objective, not whoever is richest, and players can pass it on by overtaking. That makes movement choices meaningful. The design needs an explicit, shared objective (a destination) to work.
- Itadaki's stocks reverse the usual property-game snowball: a trailing player can buy shares in the leader's district and profit from the leader's growth. Its win condition (target net worth plus return to the bank) also guarantees a bounded game length. These two are the most transferable ideas for "strategy without negotiation."
- Rich Man's "roll 1 or 2 dice" motorcycle and Machi Koro's 1-or-2-dice choice (Q3) are the same simple lever. On a 40-space board, choosing between 2–12 and 1–6 gives real positional control.
- Mario Party's random hidden bonuses are a warning: comebacks the players cannot see coming or plan for feel unfair. Announced, visible objectives (as in Itadaki or the 모두의마블 monopoly wins) avoid this.

## Q2. Monopoly and official variants (trading/auctions, house shortage, jail, Monopoly Deal, speed die, Rento)

### Takeaway
Much of Monopoly's reputation for length and luck comes from house rules that pour cash into the game, such as Free Parking money and extra pay on landing on GO. Players also commonly skip the official auction and house-shortage rules. The rules as written are shorter (about 90 minutes in tournaments) and more strategic. Hasbro's own fix for dice and length is the Speed Die, which adds move choices and a jump to the next relevant space. Monopoly Deal removes the dice and gives every turn several choices, but reviewers disagree on how much skill it involves.

### Cited Findings
- Official rules: a property the lander declines must be auctioned. Anyone may bid, including the player who declined, and bidding starts at any price. Commentators note many groups skip this, and that auctions spread properties early and make colour sets more likely, so skipping them lengthens games. — [Monopoly classic rules PDF](https://richunclepennybags.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Monopoly-classic-rules-instructions.pdf); [Wikibooks: Monopoly/House Rules](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Monopoly/House_Rules)
- House shortage: the box holds 32 houses and 12 hotels. If several players want the last house or hotel, the banker auctions it. House rules that ignore the limit remove a source of strategy. — [Wikibooks: House Rules](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Monopoly/House_Rules); [Monopoly rules (Lindholm)](https://stephenlindholm.com/monopoly_rules.html)
- Games run long because house rules inject money (Free Parking pot, $400 on GO). Official Free Parking pays nothing, and fees go to the bank. Tournament games take about 90 minutes. — [Wikibooks: House Rules](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Monopoly/House_Rules); [Xona: Monopoly championship quiz](https://xona.com/2004/07/23.html)
- Trading is the official way to complete colour sets once everything is bought, but many groups trade little. No loans except mortgages. — [Wikibooks: House Rules](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Monopoly/House_Rules)
- Speed Die (introduced in the Mega Edition 2006, standard game from 2007): used only after a player has passed GO once, and not for rolling out of jail. Faces 1–3 add to the move. Two Mr. Monopoly faces move you, after your normal move, to the next unowned property, or to the next property where you'd owe rent if all are owned. The Bus face lets you move by either die alone or their sum (for example 1-5-Bus lets you choose 1, 5 or 6). — [Wikibooks: Monopoly/The Speed Die](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Monopoly/The_Speed_Die); [Buffalo Library summary citing Hasbro instructions](https://www.buffalolib.org/sites/default/files/gaming-unplugged/desc/42%20Monopoly%20with%20Speed%20Dice%20Description.pdf)
- Monopoly Deal: one comparison calls it more strategic than Monopoly Bid, citing 12 action-card types and the popularity of "Just Say No" (cancel another player's action), and says the options let players offset bad draws. — [ExpertBeacon](https://expertbeacon.com/which-is-better-monopoly-bid-or-monopoly-deal/). A balance analysis finds several viable strategies (alliances, holding back to steal the win late), with every benefit carrying a cost. — [Mechanics of Magic: Critical Play – Monopoly Deal](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2022/05/26/critical-play-is-this-game-balanced-monopoly-deal/). Counterpoint: the strategic ceiling is low, and much of each game "lives and dies by the luck of the draw." — [Meeple Mountain: Monopoly Deal No Mercy](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/monopoly-deal-no-mercy/)

### Inferences
- For a 20–40 minute session, the evidence-backed levers are: (a) auction declined properties, which moves sets onto the board early and gives a non-roller a decision; (b) a finite building supply, which makes scarcity a reason to race and block; (c) no new money from Free Parking or similar spaces. On one tablet, auctions are easiest to run as a quick sealed or turn-around bid, not open shouting.
- The Speed Die's Bus face (choose either die or the sum) is a cheap way to give a choice on every roll, and it is easy for a CPU to evaluate. The Mr. Monopoly face speeds up the early game by steering players toward unowned property.
- Monopoly Deal's "Just Say No" defence-card pattern corresponds to 모두의마블's defence cards (shield/angel). Both need hidden hands. On one shared screen, either keep a card hand very small and reveal it only on use (with a hand-off screen), or make defence a visible, priced resource.
- Free trading needs negotiation, which is hard with pass-and-play and CPUs. Structured trade offers (CPU accepts or rejects using a valuation function) or bank-mediated swaps are more transferable.

### Gaps
- No source found on the official jail choice (pay $50 versus try for doubles) as a strategic lever, though it is in the rulebook. Rento and Monopoly Deal session lengths and exact card counts were not verified. I did not search Rento.

## Q3. Modern Euro/hybrid games (Machi Koro, Catan, Power Grid, Acquire, Takenoko; Splendor, Ticket to Ride, Lords of Waterdeep and Sushi Go not researched)

### Takeaway
Euro designs reduce dice dependence by putting the decisions around the dice, not on them. Examples: choosing how many dice to roll (Machi Koro), letting dice change actions instead of deciding outcomes (Takenoko's weather die), and putting money into shared assets whose value other players move (Acquire, like Itadaki's stocks). They limit runaway leaders with explicit, visible catch-up rules: Power Grid's reverse turn order and Catan's robber. Reviewers also show their limits. The robber hits whoever holds the most cards rather than the leader, and Machi Koro's fixed market still lets an early lucky roller run away.

### Cited Findings
- Machi Koro: reviewers say the base game is prone to runaway leaders. A player who rolls well early buys the key establishments, and "whoever creates the best engine will usually win… the only way they won't is… blind luck." The base game uses every card in every game with no setup variety. — [The Thoughtful Gamer](https://thethoughtfulgamer.com/2017/06/16/machi-koro-review/); [Board Game Quest](https://boardgamequest.com/machi-koro-review)
- The Harbor expansion limits the market to a varying set of establishments (only a selection is available at a time), which reviewers say makes runaways less likely ("still possible but the odds are lower"). Critics say Harbor nearly doubles game length (6 landmarks instead of 4), adds swingy money theft, and makes rolls above 12 possible. — [Island Ray / I Slay the Dragon review](https://islaythedragon.com/featured/bright-lights-bigger-city-a-review-of-machi-koro-harbor-expansion/); [Boardgaming.com](https://boardgaming.com/games/card-games/machi-koro-harbor-expansion); [Meeple Mountain: Machi Koro 2](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/machi-koro-2-game-review/)
- Power Grid: the player with the most cities picks power plants first but buys resources and builds last, so stronger players pay more and get the scraps. Reviewers credit this with keeping one player from running away. BGG tags it "Catch the Leader" and "Turn Order: Stat-Based." — [Board Game Meeple Lady](https://www.boardgamemeeplelady.com/?p=1171); [analog.games entry](https://analog.games/games/5a5772ef-17e6-47a0-babb-a554a6ce1a10?lang=en). A design article distinguishes catch-up mechanics, which help trailers, from "headwinds," which slow a leader through the same mechanism that makes them strong, and argues headwinds are more elegant. — [League of Gamemakers (via search summary)](https://leagueofgamemakers.com/ask-the-league-should-games-have-a-catch-up-mechanic). Power Grid Outpost is described as having "well-calibrated catch-up mechanisms." — [Board's Eye View](https://www.boardseyeview.net/post/outpost)
- Catan: on a 7, players with large hands discard, and the robber lets the roller block a hex and steal, which creates a choice about whether to target the leader. A reviewer says that apart from the robber there is no built-in catch-up, and the robber punishes anyone with many resources, not specifically the leader. Some groups adopt "nice robber" norms, and the "Cloth Thieves" fan scenario shields non-leaders from the robber. — [The Sixth Axis review](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=47827); [BGA forum](https://forum.boardgamearena.com/viewtopic.php?p=145899); [Catan Collector: Cloth Thieves](https://catancollector.com/the-cloth-thieves)
- Takenoko: a turn is 2 actions. The weather die doesn't decide success. It modifies the turn: an extra action, bamboo growth on a plot of your choice, moving the panda anywhere, a free improvement, or a joker. — [Takenoko rulebook (EN)](https://www.dover.nh.gov/Assets/government/city-operations/library/borrow/library-of-things/TAKENOKO_rulebook_EN.pdf); [Wikipedia: Takenoko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takenoko_(board_game))
- Acquire (Sid Sackson, 1962/64): you place a tile, then buy up to 3 shares. When hotel chains merge, the majority and minority shareholders of the absorbed chain get large bonuses. Other holders sell at current value or trade 2-for-1 into the survivor. Reviewers praise how placement timing interacts with buying shares. Criticisms: turn order matters more at higher player counts, and 5–6 player games run up to 2 hours. — [Miniature Market review](https://miniaturemarket.com/reviewcorner/acquire-review); [Meeple Mountain: Acquire](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/acquire/)

### Inferences
- The most transferable Euro ideas for a dice-and-property tablet game:
  - Machi Koro's "roll 1 or 2 dice," the same as Rich Man's motorcycle.
  - Takenoko-style dice-face bonuses: a special face or double grants a choice, not a fixed effect.
  - Acquire and Itadaki shares, with majority bonuses for whoever holds the most shares in a colour group.
  - A Power-Grid-style headwind tied to rank: the leader pays more to build, or acts last on a shared, limited market such as a house supply.
  All of these are fully visible information and need no negotiation, so they suit pass-and-play with CPUs.
- Catan's experience suggests a "rob the rich" effect should target the leader explicitly (by net worth), since targeting anyone with a big hand hits non-leaders too. Making the leader visibly targeted also gives trailing players a clear reason to cooperate against them without negotiating.
- Splendor, Ticket to Ride, Lords of Waterdeep and Sushi Go are not dice games. Their value here would be pattern references: card-market tempo, public objectives and drafting. They were not researched in this session.

### Gaps
- Not researched: Splendor, Ticket to Ride (destination tickets as a public or hidden objective), Lords of Waterdeep (hidden lord identities, worker placement), Sushi Go (simultaneous drafting, which is hard on one screen). No sources cited, so no claims are made.
- No measured data (simulation or win-rate statistics) for the Power Grid or Catan catch-up effects. The evidence is reviewer opinion.

## Q4. Which mechanics measurably increased strategy or comebacks (designer/review/player evidence) and what transfers to a single-tablet game

### Takeaway
No hard data surfaced. No source in this session gave win-rate or comeback statistics for any of these mechanics. The evidence is qualitative and consistent, though. Reviewers and designers credit: (a) visible headwinds on the leader (Power Grid turn order, Momotaro's Bonbi, 모두의마블 takeover); (b) shared investment in rivals' assets (Itadaki stocks, Acquire mergers); (c) bounded win conditions that end games early (Itadaki target net worth, 모두의마블 monopoly wins, Business Tour's 4-resort win); (d) small choices on each roll (Speed Die Bus, 1-or-2 dice, power gauge). Comebacks from random end-of-game bonuses (Mario Party) and swingy theft (Machi Koro Harbor) draw the most "feels unfair or too random" criticism.

### Cited Findings
- Power Grid's reverse turn order is cited by reviewers as keeping players from running away. — [Board Game Meeple Lady](https://www.boardgamemeeplelady.com/?p=1171)
- A design article prefers "headwind" designs (slowing the leader through the mechanic that makes them strong) over pure catch-up rewards. — [League of Gamemakers](https://leagueofgamemakers.com/ask-the-league-should-games-have-a-catch-up-mechanic)
- Hidden random bonuses produce comebacks that feel undeserved and can't be planned for. — [Mechanics of Magic: Mario Party](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=20809); [Zatu review](https://zatu.com/en-au/blogs/reviews/super-mario-party-review)
- Reviewers credit a limited, varying market (Machi Koro Harbor) with fewer runaways, and also say it adds length and swinginess. — [I Slay the Dragon](https://islaythedragon.com/featured/bright-lights-bigger-city-a-review-of-machi-koro-harbor-expansion/); [Meeple Mountain](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/machi-koro-2-game-review/)
- Following the rules as written (auctions, finite houses, no Free Parking money) is the documented fix for Monopoly's length. Tournament games take about 90 minutes. — [Wikibooks: House Rules](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Monopoly/House_Rules)
- Softstar calls Richman's card system the series' "most strategic system." — [Steam: Richman 11](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2074800/)
- Momotaro's poverty god punishes the player farthest from the goal and moves by overtaking. — [djcube](https://djcube.co.uk/?p=48375); [Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20201121-momotetsu-teiban-review)
- Dokapon shows the downside of making player-versus-player harm central with no anti-runaway brake: with 2 players a leader widens the gap, and campaigns run over 25 hours. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/dokapon-kingdom-review); [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/18453)

### Inferences
Transferability ranking for a single-tablet, pass-and-play game with CPUs and 20–40 minute sessions (my synthesis, not from sources):
1. **Easy to transfer, high value** (visible information, no negotiation, CPU-friendly):
   - Takeover at a multiple of build cost, plus a landmark lock (모두의마블).
   - Alternative instant wins (line / triple / tourist), which force blocking.
   - An escalating host-city multiplier with a cap (Olympics / World Championship).
   - A choice on each roll (Bus-style die choice, 1-or-2 dice, or a visible power gauge).
   - A world-tour space that lets you teleport by choice.
   - Auctions for declined properties (sealed bid on tablet).
   - A finite building supply.
   - Rank-based headwinds (the leader pays more or acts last).
   - A leader- or laggard-targeted NPC (a Bonbi-like debuff that moves when overtaken).
   - District shares with majority payouts (Itadaki / Acquire).
   - A target-net-worth-plus-return-to-start win condition, to bound session length.
2. **Medium** (needs UI care):
   - Small card hands of attack and defence cards (모두의마블 Fortune cards, Rich Man cards, Monopoly Deal "Just Say No"). These need a hidden hand on a shared screen. Mitigate with a hand-off curtain, or by making cards one-shot and used right away when drawn.
   - Stock price movement driven by trades.
3. **Hard or poor fit:**
   - Free-form trading and negotiation, alliance cards (Rich Man's 7-day alliance), simultaneous drafting (Sushi Go).
   - Long campaigns and multi-year modes (Momotaro 99 years, Dokapon 25+ hours).
   - Random hidden end bonuses (Mario Party), which risk feeling unfair, especially when CPUs sweep them.
   - Monetised dice multipliers (Monopoly GO): a retention mechanic, not strategy.

### Gaps
- No quantitative studies, GDC talks, or designer post-mortems on Monopoly-like comeback tuning (for example from Netmarble, Hudson/Konami or Square Enix) were found in this session. A follow-up could look for Korean developer interviews (인벤, 디스이즈게임) on 모두의마블 balancing, and Japanese interviews with Horii on Itadaki.
- No measured session lengths for 모두의마블 matches (commonly said to be about 10–20 minutes, but unsourced), Business Tour, or Rich Man.
